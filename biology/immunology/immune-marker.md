# Immune Markers

Immune markers (or immunological biomarkers) are measurable molecules, proteins, or cellular characteristics that reflect the state or activity of the immune system. They are widely used in research, diagnostics, and clinical practice to evaluate **immune response, disease progression, and therapeutic effectiveness**.

## Categories of Immune Markers

### 1. **Cell Surface Markers**

- Molecules expressed on the surface of immune cells.
- Identified by **cluster of differentiation (CD) molecules**.
- Examples:
  - **CD3** → T lymphocytes (pan-T cell marker)
  - **CD4** → Helper T cells
  - **CD8** → Cytotoxic T cells
  - **CD19 / CD20** → B cells
  - **CD56** → Natural Killer (NK) cells

### 2. **Cytokines & Chemokines**

- Secreted proteins that mediate immune communication.
- Examples:
  - **IL-2, IL-6, IL-10** (interleukins)
  - **TNF-α** (tumor necrosis factor alpha)
  - **IFN-γ** (interferon gamma)
  - **CXCL8 (IL-8)** → attracts neutrophils

### 3. **Functional Markers**

- Reflect cell activation, proliferation, or cytotoxicity.
- Examples:
  - **Ki-67** → proliferation marker
  - **CD69** → early T-cell activation
  - **CD25** (IL-2 receptor α) → T-cell activation
  - **Granzyme B, Perforin, CD107a (LAMP-1)** → cytotoxic activity

### 4. **Immune Checkpoint Molecules**

- Inhibitory receptors and ligands that regulate immune responses, especially T-cell activity.
- Examples:
  - **PD-1 (CD279)** → receptor on activated T cells; marker of T-cell exhaustion.
  - **PD-L1 (CD274, B7-H1)** → ligand expressed on APCs and tumor cells; suppresses T-cell activity.
  - **CTLA-4 (CD152)** → inhibitory receptor on T cells that competes with CD28 for B7 ligands.
- **Clinical relevance:** Immune checkpoint markers are widely used as **biomarkers in cancer** and targeted by **checkpoint inhibitor therapies** (e.g., anti-PD-1, anti-PD-L1, anti-CTLA-4).

### 5. **Antibodies & Serum Proteins**

- Indicators of humoral immunity.
- Examples:
  - **IgG, IgA, IgM** → immunoglobulin isotypes
  - **Complement components (C3, C4)** → innate immunity
  - **C-reactive protein (CRP)** → acute-phase inflammation marker

## Applications of Immune Markers

- **Disease diagnosis**
  - HIV: CD4+ T-cell count
  - Lymphoma: aberrant CD marker expression
- **Treatment monitoring**
  - Immunotherapy response (e.g., PD-1/PD-L1 expression)
- **Research**
  - Flow cytometry profiling of immune subsets
  - ELISA/ELISPOT to quantify cytokine release
