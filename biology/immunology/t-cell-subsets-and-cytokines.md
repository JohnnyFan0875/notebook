# T Cell Subsets and Cytokines

## 1. Major Immune Cell Classes and Functions

| Category                            | Representative Cell Types                                                             | Functional Overview                                                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Innate Immunity**                 | Macrophages, Dendritic Cells (DC), Neutrophils, Natural Killer (NK) Cells, Mast Cells | Rapid response, phagocytosis, inflammation, and early antiviral defense  |
| **Adaptive Immunity**               | T cells (CD4⁺ helper T, CD8⁺ cytotoxic T), B cells (B → plasma cell)                  | Antigen-specific immunity, antibody production, and immunological memory |
| **Regulatory and Supportive Cells** | Regulatory T cells (Treg), Follicular Helper T cells (Tfh), Antigen-presenting DCs    | Maintain immune tolerance, assist B cell maturation                      |

## 2. CD4⁺ T Helper Subsets and Representative Cytokines

| T Cell Subset                 | Inducing Factors / Differentiation Environment | Major Secreted Cytokines | Primary Functions                                                             | Key Transcription Factors | Related Diseases                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **TH1**                       | IL-12, IFN-γ                                   | IFN-γ, IL-2, TNF-α       | Activates macrophages and CTLs; antiviral and intracellular bacterial defense | **T-bet**, STAT4          | Tuberculosis defense, autoimmune diseases (MS, T1DM) |
| **TH2**                       | IL-4                                           | IL-4, IL-5, IL-13        | Humoral immunity, anti-parasite defense, IgE promotion, allergy               | **GATA3**, STAT6          | Asthma, atopic dermatitis, helminth infection        |
| **TH17**                      | IL-6, TGF-β, IL-23                             | IL-17A, IL-17F, IL-22    | Recruits neutrophils, antifungal and extracellular bacterial defense          | **RORγt**, STAT3          | Psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis, IBD                 |
| **Treg** (Regulatory T)       | TGF-β, IL-2                                    | IL-10, TGF-β             | Suppresses immune activation, maintains tolerance                             | **FoxP3**, STAT5          | Autoimmunity if defective                            |
| **Tfh** (Follicular Helper T) | IL-6, IL-21                                    | IL-21, IL-4              | Supports B cell differentiation and antibody affinity maturation              | **Bcl6**, STAT3           | SLE, chronic viral infection                         |
| **TH9**                       | IL-4, TGF-β                                    | IL-9                     | Enhances eosinophil and mast cell responses                                   | **PU.1**, IRF4            | Allergic asthma, parasite immunity                   |
| **TH22**                      | IL-6, TNF-α                                    | IL-22                    | Promotes epithelial repair and mucosal defense                                | **AHR**, STAT3            | Psoriasis, dermatitis                                |
| **γδ T cell**                 | Constitutive or infection-induced              | IFN-γ, IL-17             | Mucosal defense, tumor surveillance                                           | Distinct from αβ T cells  | Mucosal inflammation, cancer defense                 |

## 6. Summary

- **TH1 / TH2 / TH17 / Treg / Tfh / TH9 / TH22** constitute the major CD4⁺ T helper differentiation pathways.
- **Key balancing cytokines:** IFN-γ, IL-4, IL-10, IL-17, IL-22, and TGF-β.
- The **equilibrium and deviation** among these lineages determine whether immunity leads to infection clearance, allergy, or autoimmunity.
- Modern immunotherapies—such as checkpoint inhibitors and cytokine blockade—are designed based on these signaling networks.
